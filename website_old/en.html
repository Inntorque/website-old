Robotics applications

Joint torque feedback for responsive robot motion

From human–robot collaboration to joint development, explore how torque measurement supports motion, contact sensing and load monitoring across robotic tasks.

Collaborative robots

Monitor interactions between robot arms, people and workpieces during machine tending, material handling and hand-guided teaching.

The role of torque measurementProvide joint load feedback to support hand-guided teaching, compliant control and detection of unexpected contact.

Humanoid robots

Explore grasping, coordinated two-arm manipulation and lower-limb motion, with insight into forces and load distribution across multiple joints.

The role of torque measurementSupply measured data for joint torque and whole-body control algorithms, supporting motion tuning and load evaluation.

Assistive exoskeletons

Address assistance needs at the hip, knee and elbow during walking, lifting and repetitive tasks with wearable devices.

The role of torque measurementMeasure joint output and interaction torque to inform assistance adjustment, motion tracking and human–machine interaction research.

Precision assembly & insertion

Track load changes caused by jamming or misalignment during connector insertion, press-fitting and precision part assembly.

The role of torque measurementAnalyze joint torque changes alongside robot motion models to support contact detection and assembly process monitoring.

Grinding, polishing & finishing

Monitor contact and resistance as tools move over workpieces during curved-surface grinding, edge deburring and polishing.

The role of torque measurementProvide joint load feedback to support contact assessment, compliant trajectory control and process tuning.

Joint module development & testing

Characterize motors, reducers and integrated joints on test benches across different speeds, poses and loads.

The role of torque measurementCapture output torque and load changes to support transmission evaluation, controller tuning and product consistency testing.

Technical capabilities

Three non-contact measurement systems

Magnetoelastic torque measurement · Near-field telemetry · Wireless measurement systems

Magnetoelastic measurement

Measured variables: torque, angle, acceleration, temperature

  • Measurement distance: < 2 mm
  • Sampling rate: > 25 kHz
  • Minimal radial installation space
  • No additional components on the shaft
  • Fully automated series production

Near-field telemetry

Measured variables: torque, bending, axial force, acceleration, angle

  • Transmission distance: 1–35 mm
  • Wireless power and data transfer
  • Sampling rate: up to 5 kHz
  • Suitable for all material types
  • For research and laboratory applications

Long-range Bluetooth

Versatile wireless transmission

  • Transmission distance: up to 15 m
  • Sampling rate: up to 500 Hz
  • Conventional or battery power
  • Compatible with multiple sensor types
  • (strain gauges, acceleration, temperature and more)
